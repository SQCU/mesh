# Metal publication words in compiler-allocated memory, November 23, 2026

**A:** The compiler placed two arrays next to each other. The last GPU group writing a range stored its generation in the first available word for that range with Metal’s `coherent(system)` qualifier. On completing the last range, it also advanced the whole-result generation with a device-scope atomic maximum. In the two-node encoder, the CPU polled byte 606760 and that same GPU dispatch updated byte 606728. Both belonged to the same cache line. In one invocation the GPU finished the publishing dispatch about 0.35 ms after its first timestamp. The CPU spun until about 160 ms before it observed the new generation. It then copied and posted the 8 KiB range in about 1 µs. **B:** The compiler places the read-only transfer records between the array of whole-result generations and the array of per-range generations. Each producer and consumer uses the offset of its original word. This separates the words touched by the two forms of GPU store while preserving concurrent generation updates and the ability to send each range immediately. Compilation changes the byte offsets. The executable performs the same numerical work, publication, and bridge writes. [State layout, revision 8db4ad45b](../rdma/mesh-collective.c)

With that layout, the slow root dispatch that consumes the peer’s reply took a median of 72 µs over 12 observed calls. The 24 ordinary calls returned in a median of 0.99 ms. The 24-call batch ran in 18.24 ms and drained outstanding page transfers in another 16 µs. Both checks matched the eager tensor within a maximum absolute error of 7.16 × 10⁻⁷. The measurements identify a causal effect of placing these arrays on separate cache lines. They do not distinguish GPU cache writeback from the other hardware mechanisms involved in visibility.
