# Prepared native crossings

These are the emitted instructions in `rdma/.build/mesh-flow.s`, produced by `make -C rdma native-audit` with `-O2 -Wall -Wextra -Werror`. They describe the current native transport, not the deleted Swift/generated numerical executor. They do not establish crossing latency or distributed speedup. Provider-internal instructions and native numerical shader instructions are not counted as zero.

M08 requests user-interactive QoS in `pthread_create` attributes and destroys those attributes before preparation completes. There is no policy read or call in either progress loop; emitted one-stream accepted paths contain the six loads below. The one-stream selection occurs at thread entry. For multiple independent streams, the loop still loads the selected record’s QP, then additionally stores the accepted successor at its prepared cursor position and loads the next stream's cursor; the number of probes is bounded by configured streams times invocation concurrency, not the 35-layer graph. No later receive can change the prepared send order. Empty polling checks the explicit shutdown word; accepted publications do not read it before posting.

The receive destination/value and next-request/QP fields occupy one aligned 32-byte line at +64 within the 128-byte receive record. Native TX discovery depth is 0 for one stream; native RX discovery depth is 1 from `wr_id`. TX has two accepted-path load instructions, both from one M04 cell. Its QP is loaded once before polling because the prepared stream fixes that queue for every invocation; the compiler retains it in x20 across native posts. RX has four through repost: two from the native completion and two from its directly addressed record. Neither accepted path reloads a spilled register. The provider's own loads, coherence cost, and full store-to-consumer latency remain unmeasured.

The GPU handoff is inside the actual numerical kernel in the prepared indirect program. Entry-prefix compilation and selection happen only during recording; no compiler description is traversed at execution. Each requested step has a prepared native range with distinct completion bindings, while pipelines and numerical storage remain shared. The requested steps and M13 boundary copies are submitted together; numerical dispatches are still replayed, not across-step resident. An accepted CQ publishes the actual transport completion; no additional generation identity is computed. A new generation cannot reuse a layer contribution before both ranks have consumed the preceding step's dependent layer chain. This graph fact is not implemented as a reuse guard. Paired execution is recorded in D0; it does not establish the required speedup or crossing latency. Two-layer static-input experiments in engine `output_data/e2b-rdma-replay-20260919/two-layer-entry-experiments.json` compare SIMD-width consumer traversal (279.52 us), shared command ranges (284.81 us), and restored complete ranges (279.63 us); adjacent unsplit solo is 262.19 us. All clients retire normally. The SIMD traversal and split-range encoder are deleted in `c7a1846` and `b7ca7fb`: fewer threads or fewer stored commands established no latency improvement. These are two-layer interval observations, not wire latency or a full-model speedup; native T1/T2 remain seven loads at depths 0/1.

This boundary table states the shader source accesses, not an emitted GPU instruction count. It is outside both RDMA crossing transitions. After submission the host only observes completion of the requested interval and reads its outputs. For an original layer interval (`prepare_e2b.py --layers LO:HI`), M01 initializes static boundary operands before recording; M06 contains only those layers, with the same publication and receive lowering. The replay client constructs no M13 token/state operands or decode-advance commands for that interval. No layer-selection branch, input initializer or numerical timing operation executes in T1/T2.

M26 is outside both crossing transitions and outside decode steps. `mesh_transfers_start` stores the prepared client, advances the shared notification word and wakes the bridge main thread; `mesh_retire` stores the released client and notifies; explicit bridge shutdown advances the same word before wake. Main retains the notification value before reading control state and uses Apple's atomic compare-and-wait, so notification before sleep is observed without a timer. The native progress threads retain the same accepted-path load instructions and independent continuous polling.

M22 also samples paired CPU/GPU clocks before submission and after retirement, inside raw CPU-clock brackets. These reads are outside execution, diagnostic-only, and do not alter a SEND, CQ event or shader. Local event alignment must retain their measured uncertainty and check the native counter scale; clocks on different nodes are not aligned.

M22 diagnostic samples are native timestamp commands with indices and command-range cuts realized during preparation. `TRACE_CROSSINGS` samples one crossing per step and exposes the numerical-region, publication-dispatch and post-publication-to-first-numerical-consumer intervals; a fused publication/consumer is reported as one combined interval, without inferring its internal split; its precise samples perturb the sampled step and do not establish an H7 pass. With the option absent, no cut storage, subrange replay or additional timestamp is submitted; M20 remains the two endpoint samples.

For M28 the publication cut brackets the original numerical producer, which now publishes internally; its measured dispatch interval would include the projection arithmetic. It cannot be interpreted as publication overhead. Ordinary endpoint observations in engine `output_data/e2b-rdma-replay-20260919/two-layer-final-output/result.json` record static layers 4:6 only: final SIMD-writer publication at 256.491 and 256.113 us against adjacent engine `2210d85` runs at 260.179 and 266.014 us. Per-value atomics at 261.238 us are superseded by SIMD aggregation. Engine `e59488d` retains 75 commands and 57 numerical dependency barriers per invocation. Explicit cancellation returns 89/89; the following normal pair returns 0/0 at 256.778 us. These observations establish neither the full-model 1.10 objective nor an H7 crossing budget. Engine `3f4eafb` also compiles the writer count and ownership before execution: `output_data/e2b-rdma-replay-20260919/prepared-writers/result.json` records original static layers 14:16, covering both INT4 and INT2. The new pair takes 251.372 and 253.853 us against adjacent old-code observations of 258.074 and 256.352 us; all clients return zero. The matched unsplit solo is 241.103 us, so the pair remains slower. The saved baseline library is verified through its generated shader. No full-model speedup or H7 pass follows from this two-layer observation; native seven-load T1/T2 is unchanged.

M27 is compiled only with `MESH_TRACE=1`. TX reads the local CPU clock after accepting readiness, before calling the provider and after return, then writes one 32-byte diagnostic record. RX reads that clock after a nonempty poll, after the completion store and after repost, then writes its record. Trace bases and extents are allocated in `link_prepare`; identities and timestamps are printed in `link_close` only after progress threads are joined. These timestamp reads and record writes are diagnostic perturbations, not production-path operations or H7 pass evidence. Without the compile definition the arrays, clocks, stores and printing do not exist.

Offline placement solving adds no T1/T2 instruction. `tools/mesh/solve_placement.py` reads repeated M27 native publication and arrival records after retirement, averages their elapsed work intervals, selects measured widths with binary HiGHS variables under the cyclic arrival-dependency constraints, and emits caller shares. Unmeasured timing interpolation, adjacent-segment selectors and the separate alignment variables are deleted. Predicted phase offsets remain solver output; they never become runtime delays or a schedule. The existing graph preparer realizes those shares and discards them before execution. Native event identities and caller backend labels are offline inputs only; no profile lookup, solver call or branch selects a shard at runtime. Engine `output_data/e2b-rdma-replay-20260919/two-layer-measured-solver/result.json` records the two-layer observations: interpolated widths take 275.908 us; measured-width choices take 263.272 and 259.959 us versus the adjacent preceding placement at 260.413 us. No placement speedup is established. The 246.503 us deterministic prediction remains below the observed intervals and is not substituted for them.
